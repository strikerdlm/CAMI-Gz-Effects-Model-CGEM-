# Reviewer Report — Methodology (Conformal / ML / UQ)
**Reviewer:** Anonymous Reviewer 1
**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Recommendation:** Major Revisions

## Summary of the manuscript

The manuscript proposes an additive machine-learning wrapper around a validated mechanistic ODE physiological model — the FAA CAMI G-Effects Model (CGEM) of acceleration physiology — without modifying the validated Fortran core. The wrapper has four components: (i) per-target XGBoost surrogate emulators (two-stage classifier + regressor for right-censored event-time targets, single-stage for continuous targets) trained on a 3,240-row deterministic synthetic dataset derived from the binary; (ii) a Mondrian split-conformal calibration layer stratified by maneuver category, replaced by Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) on the long-tailed `time_to_gloc_s` target via an OSF amendment dated 2026-05-06; (iii) a robust-Mahalanobis out-of-distribution detector with distribution-free conformal abstention over a 17-dimensional mixed numeric/binary/ordinal feature space; and (iv) Sobol/Morris global sensitivity decomposition driven through the surrogate.

The reported empirical anchors on the OSF-pre-registered held-out test split are: classifier AUROC ≥ 0.996 (ECE ≤ 0.014); regressor R² of 0.82–0.90 on the event-positive slice of censored targets and 0.94–1.00 on the two continuous targets; conformal OOD in-envelope rate of 0.953 against a nominal 0.95; Mondrian conformal coverage on the four retained targets between 0.928 and 0.967; CQR coverage on `time_to_gloc_s` of 0.972 (improving on a homoscedastic Mondrian baseline of 0.861 on n = 36 event-positive rows); ~180× wall-clock acceleration over direct CGEM subprocess invocation; and `g_peak_abs` as the dominant Sobol total-order driver of all three event-time targets. An external H6 arm against an n = 8 archival cohort declared in the cover letter and §3.7 as a pre-registered "partial failure": the surrogate is in-bracket at onset ≥ 1 G/s but exhibits a δ̄ = +26.6 s [95 % CI +6.3, +52.1] slow-onset bias at onset ≤ 0.5 G/s.

The contribution is positioned as a methodological extension of Portela, Banga & Matabuena (2025), with three operational refinements — Mondrian stratification over operationally meaningful sub-populations, a heteroscedastic CQR layer for the long-tailed event-time target, and a distribution-free conformal abstention layer over a robust-Mahalanobis OOD score — together with a regulatory-aerospace demonstration substrate, an OSF pre-registration, an open Zenodo dataset, a Docker reproducibility image, and a FastAPI service.

## Major concerns

1. **Pre-registration chain on the CQR layer (H5) is not described accurately.** The manuscript repeatedly states that the CQR layer was locked "before any test-set evaluation under the new layer" (§2.4; cover letter §"What is known…"). That phrasing, taken literally, implies the test split was untouched at the time of OSF amendment H5 (2026-05-06). The amendment file (`docs/publication/osf_amendment_2026-05-06.md` §B-H5) tells a different story: it explicitly anchors CQR's success criterion against the value "0.861 on the same test slice (`docs/publication/manuscript.md` §3.3, Table 2, commit 1f1a816)." That number is a test-set Mondrian coverage that, by definition, was computed before the amendment was filed. The original pre-registration (`docs/publication/osf_preregistration.md` §1, H2) is even more explicit: it lists the 9-pp `time_to_gloc_s` under-coverage as an *empirical anchor* and pre-emptively names "heteroscedastic conformal extension (Romano et al. 2019)" as the remedy. Read together, the chain is: (a) Mondrian was evaluated on the test split at "Phase-3 smoke run before posting"; (b) the under-coverage was named in the prereg with Romano et al. 2019 as the proposed remedy; (c) CQR was then locked under amendment H5 with the test-set baseline in hand; (d) CQR was evaluated on the same test split. This is **not data peeking** in the strict sense — the remedy was named in the original prereg — but it is **not the standard "blind locking" semantics** that the current manuscript wording implies. **Suggested fix:** rewrite §2.4 (Conformal — CQR paragraph) and §2.7 to describe the chain plainly: "The homoscedastic Mondrian under-coverage of 0.861 on the test split was already reported as the empirical anchor of H2 in the original OSF pre-registration, which also named CQR (Romano et al. 2019) as the candidate remedy. OSF amendment H5 (2026-05-06) formalised CQR as the primary layer for `time_to_gloc_s` with knowledge of that anchor; the CQR layer itself was not fit on the test split, only evaluated against it." Drop the standalone "before any test-set evaluation" formulation from §2.4 and the cover letter; it overclaims and a careful reviewer will catch the chain via the amendment file.

2. **The Mondrian/CQR stratification is degenerate on `time_to_gloc_s` — the headline result is essentially a single-stratum claim.** Table 2 (line 169) shows the CQR row distribution across maneuver categories: championship n = 1, conceptual 0/0, extreme post-stall 0/0, military ACM n = 35. The overall n = 36 is therefore 97 % military ACM. A Mondrian per-`maneuver_category` quantile *reduces to a global quantile on that single stratum*; the same is true of the CQR per-stratum conformity scoring. The CQR-vs-Mondrian comparison the manuscript draws (0.861 → 0.972) is essentially "on one stratum (military ACM) with n = 35 event-positive test rows." The Clopper–Pearson CIs reported below the table ([0.706, 0.949] Mondrian vs [0.847, 0.999] CQR) **overlap**, which the authors do note ("operationally closer to nominal rather than statistically dominant"). But the table caption, the abstract, and the §3.3 narrative ("All five (target, stage) pairs achieve overall coverage within ±5 pp of nominal") sell this as a clean Mondrian/CQR per-category result, which it is not. **Suggested fix:** (a) in §3.3 and Table 2's caption, state explicitly that the `time_to_gloc_s` regressor row is operationally a single-stratum (military-ACM) claim with n = 35; (b) reframe H5 as "CQR raises overall coverage on the military-ACM stratum of `time_to_gloc_s` from 0.857 to 0.971" rather than as a five-target headline; (c) in the abstract, change "CQR raised `time_to_gloc_s` coverage from 0.861 to 0.972 (n = 36 event-positive)" to "CQR raised `time_to_gloc_s` coverage from 0.861 to 0.972 on n = 36 event-positive rows, 35 of which are military ACM"; (d) acknowledge in §4.4 that the cross-stratum claim of "preserves coverage within operational sub-populations" is currently only demonstrated for the two Mondrian-retained continuous targets (Table 2 rows 1–2, where four strata are populated with non-trivial n), not for the CQR-rescued target. The Mondrian-as-implementation-detail vs Mondrian-as-empirically-supported-claim distinction matters because the cover letter sells the Mondrian stratification as one of three operational refinements.

3. **Abstract numerical inconsistency: "Mondrian coverage was within 4.6 pp on 4/5 targets" is not reproducible from Table 2.** Computing absolute deviations from nominal 0.95 on the five Mondrian rows of Table 2: `hlap_min` 0.928 → 2.2 pp; `c_bank_min` 0.949 → 0.1 pp; `time_to_greyout_s` regressor 1.000 → 5.0 pp (over-cover, n = 84); `time_to_blackout_s` regressor 1.000 → 5.0 pp (over-cover, n = 58); `time_to_gloc_s` Mondrian 0.861 → 8.9 pp; CQR 0.972 → 2.2 pp. If "Mondrian" is read narrowly as the four targets where Mondrian is retained (rows 1, 2, 4, 6 of Table 2), the four pp values are {2.2, 0.1, 5.0, 5.0}, with max 5.0 — not 4.6. If the three classifier rows (0.967, 0.953, 0.940) are included as well, max absolute deviation is 1.7 pp. Either way, **no plausible reading of Table 2 yields "within 4.6 pp."** **Suggested fix:** state the worst Mondrian-retained deviation explicitly. The honest claim is something like "Mondrian retained coverage within 5 pp of nominal on the four targets where it is the primary layer; over-covered to 1.000 on n = 84 and n = 58 event-positive slices of greyout and blackout respectively; CQR replaces Mondrian on `time_to_gloc_s` and lands at 0.972 (2.2 pp above nominal)." Also reconcile §4.1 ("overall coverage is within 5 pp of nominal on all five (target, stage) pairs once CQR replaces the homoscedastic Mondrian"): this is true at 5.0 pp on greyout and blackout regressor rows, which is at the boundary of the ±5 pp pre-registered tolerance and worth stating, not eliding.

4. **H6 external-validation bootstrap on N = 8 has high leverage; a leave-one-out sensitivity is missing.** The δ̄ = +26.6 s [+6.3, +52.1] bootstrap CI on N = 8 is driven by three slow-onset rows with very large residuals (+81.0, +71.4, +56.4 at onsets 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 G/s; Table 5). Bootstrap on N = 8 is itself inherently unstable (the empirical distribution is undersampled relative to bootstrap-resample variability), and a sensitivity to (a) bootstrap seed and (b) leave-one-out δ̄ recomputation is not reported. The "operational scope ≥ 1 G/s in-bracket" defence (§3.7, §4.4, §5) is convincing on its face — the four onset ≥ 1 G/s rows are all in-bracket with |δ| ≤ 1.3 s — but the slow-onset bias's CI is sufficiently dependent on three rows that a real reviewer will ask: what is δ̄ with each of {(0.05 G/s, +81.0 s), (0.10 G/s, +71.4 s), (0.20 G/s, +56.4 s)} held out one at a time? **Suggested fix:** add a one-paragraph LOO sensitivity to §3.7 (or supplementary Data S7), reporting δ̄ with each of the three slow-onset rows held out and at least the bootstrap-seed sensitivity (re-run with seeds 0, 42, 12345 and report range). The current text labels the result a "partial external-validation failure with a well-defined operational scope," which is honest framing; the LOO addition simply removes the residual concern that any one of the three driver rows is mis-transcribed from the source table. The Phase A cohort being pooled mean ± SD (not per-subject) makes this LOO probe especially cheap and especially informative.

5. **The Mondrian/CQR conformity-score formula in §2.4 is stated in two equivalent forms that read as redundant.** "the per-stratum conformal quantile is `q̂_s = Q((1 − α)(1 + 1/n_s), residuals_s)`, with finite-sample correction `ceil((n_s+1)(1−α)) / n_s`" — these two expressions are the same finite-sample correction written in two notations (the first is `(1 − α) × (n_s + 1) / n_s` which equals `((n_s+1)(1−α)) / n_s` once you note that `Q` operates on a discrete set of `n_s` residuals so the level is implicitly ceilinged). The CQR paragraph then refers to "the same finite-sample correction as the homoscedastic Mondrian layer" without restating it. A clean reformulation: state the correction once as `q̂_s = residual_{(⌈(n_s+1)(1−α)⌉)}` (per Boström, Johansson & Löfström 2018) and reuse the same statement for both Mondrian and CQR layers. **Suggested fix:** condense to one canonical formula citation; the current dual statement reads as a defensive over-specification that obscures what is otherwise a textbook construction.

6. **Mondrian small-stratum break-down is not bounded in the manuscript.** §2.4 states "Rows from strata unseen during calibration receive the global quantile." That is the standard fallback. But the manuscript does not discuss: (a) what happens at calibration strata with very small `n_s` — for `n_s ≤ ⌈1/α⌉ − 1 = 19` at α = 0.05 the order-statistic position `⌈(n_s + 1)(1 − α)⌉` equals `n_s`, so the per-stratum Mondrian "quantile" is literally the maximum absolute residual on that stratum, and coverage is structurally forced to 1.0; (b) for which strata in the present synthetic dataset Mondrian over- vs under-coverage is structurally forced by `n_s` rather than learned. The `time_to_gloc_s` Mondrian row (overall n = 36 test, with the *calibration*-set n on each stratum unreported in Table 2 — only test n is shown) is the cleanest example: `time_to_gloc_s` is the rarest event and its event-positive calibration n per stratum is plausibly in the saturation regime on every non-military-ACM stratum, which would explain the championship n = 1 cell at 1.000 in Table 2 row 8. The `time_to_greyout_s` (n = 84 test) and `time_to_blackout_s` (n = 58 test) regressor rows showing overall coverage = 1.000 are more likely an absolute-residual concentration effect on a deterministic synthetic dataset than finite-sample saturation, and should be analysed as such rather than conflated. **Suggested fix:** add one paragraph to §2.4 quantifying the saturation regime (`n_s ≤ ⌈1/α⌉ − 1`) and one column to Table 2 (or a supplementary Table) reporting the calibration `n_s` per (target, stratum); flag strata where coverage is structurally bounded at 1.0 by the finite-sample correction rather than empirically. This matters operationally because a coverage of 1.000 looks like calibration success but is, on those n's, indistinguishable from "the quantile equals the max residual."

7. **The two-stage `P(event) × E[time | event]` decomposition: censoring-mechanism assumption is not stated.** §2.4 ("Model architecture") presents the two-stage pattern as a pragmatic shortcut to the principled conformalized-survival approach (Candès, Lei & Ren 2023; Gui, Hannig & Hofmann 2024; Davidov et al. 2025). The shortcut is reasonable as a paper-1 simplification, but its validity rests on **conditional independence of censoring and outcome given covariates** — i.e., on the synthetic dataset, an event must be censored at the maneuver-window end only because the profile is too short to reach G-LOC, not because the maneuver itself is selectively shorter on subjects who would have G-LOC'd later. On a synthetic dataset generated from a deterministic CGEM run, that conditional independence is *by construction* (the censoring time is the profile duration, which is a covariate). The manuscript does not make this argument; it should. **Suggested fix:** add one paragraph to §2.4 stating that censoring is a deterministic function of `profile_duration_s` (a covariate), so conditional independence of censoring and outcome holds by construction in the synthetic regime, and explicitly flag that this assumption will not transfer cleanly to archival or own-centrifuge cohorts (paper-2 / paper-3 scope), motivating the conformalized-survival upgrade.

8. **Mahalanobis on mixed continuous/binary/ordinal features: the abstention layer's coverage guarantee is correct, but the OOD score's *operational semantics* are not.** §2.5 routes the operational threshold through distribution-free conformal abstention (a correct construction — the (1 − α) in-envelope rate on exchangeable in-distribution data is guaranteed regardless of Mahalanobis misspecification, this is well-stated). The remaining concern is **what kinds of distribution shift Mahalanobis actually picks up in this mixed-feature space**: small dehydration shifts (continuous) vs novel `who_profile` mass distribution shifts (binary mass migration) vs interaction shifts (continuous × `cm_ordinal`). The LOGO analysis in §3.5 + Table 4 partially addresses this — held-out category AUROC is 0.387–0.659 with overlapping CIs between Mahalanobis and Isolation Forest — but the manuscript reads the result as "this is a finding about dataset structure rather than detector failure," which is fair but stops short of telling the reader what *will* trigger the OOD flag in deployment. **Suggested fix:** add a short table or paragraph to §3.5 listing 3–5 plausible operational shifts (e.g., dehydration > 0.10, `who_profile` mass moving entirely to `who_5`, `g_peak_abs` extrapolation to 11–12 G, novel countermeasure combinations) and reporting whether each crosses the conformal abstention threshold on a synthetic probe set. This gives operators a deployment-time intuition for what the abstention layer actually guards.

9. **Methodological delta vs Portela et al. (2025) is real but should be quantified, not just listed.** §1 (line 25) and §4.3 (line 273) list the four extensions to Portela 2025 — additive wrap, Mondrian stratification, heteroscedastic CQR, distribution-free conformal abstention. The list is accurate. PMEA's bar is methodological rigor + reproducibility + scope-fit, not Nature-tier novelty, so the delta does not need to be enormous. However, a reviewer who is also a Portela reader will ask: "do any of these four extensions matter empirically on the data presented, or are they just architectural choices?" The answer in the present manuscript is mixed: (a) additive wrap is structural, not empirical; (b) Mondrian stratification is empirically vacuous on `time_to_gloc_s` (single dominant stratum, see major concern 2), demonstrably useful on `hlap_min` and `c_bank_min`; (c) CQR is empirically substantial (0.861 → 0.972) but the empirical anchor for the comparison is degenerate (concern 2 again); (d) conformal abstention's correction over χ²(17, 0.95) is the cleanest empirical anchor (37.2 % false-positive rate → 4.7 %, §3.5 line 197), and this should be foregrounded as the strongest methodological contribution. **Suggested fix:** in §4.3, reorder the four extensions so the conformal-abstention vs χ² result (with the 37.2 % vs 4.7 % anchor) leads; demote Mondrian stratification to "supports per-stratum reporting on the continuous targets where strata are populated" rather than as a co-equal extension.

10. **Pre-registered Sobol convergence threshold (CI width ≤ 0.05 on top-3 ST features) is not reported in §3.6 against the threshold.** §2.7 lists "Sobol convergence (bootstrap CI width ≤ 0.05 on the top-3 ST features)" as a pre-registered success criterion. §3.6 reports point ST values and qualitative findings but does not state the bootstrap CI widths of the top-3 ST features against that 0.05 threshold. The supplementary Data S3 may carry the CIs but the main text does not test the H4a anchor cleanly. **Suggested fix:** add a one-line statement to §3.6: "Bootstrap CI widths on the top-3 ST features per target are ≤ X.XXX (max across the five targets); the H4a convergence criterion of CI width ≤ 0.05 is met for {targets}; H4a is not met for {targets}." If H4a is met everywhere, say so; if it is not met on `time_to_gloc_s` (which would be unsurprising given the long-tail signal), declare it as a documented exception consistent with the pre-registered failure-handling rule.

## Minor concerns

1. **§3.6 line 222** — ST = 1.005 with bootstrap CI spanning [0.92, 1.07] is described as "finite-sample noise from the N = 1,024 Saltelli sample, not a violation of Sobol's variance decomposition." This is correct, but Sobol indices > 1 reliably surface in Saltelli sampling on near-deterministic outputs (`hlap_min` is essentially deterministic in dehydration_level by construction). State it cleanly: when the output variance is concentrated on one input and Y(x) is near-deterministic in that input, the Saltelli estimator's variance is comparable to the total variance and ST estimates straddle 1.0 by sample noise. A two-sentence footnote will do.

2. **§3.3 line 169** — Table 2's `time_to_gloc_s` CQR row reports n = 1 for championship and "0/0" for two strata. The conceptual stratum n = 21 has 0 event-positive rows. The table caption already flags ⚠️ for n < 20 but the championship n = 1 cell is also flagged ⚠️; suggest a "‡" marker to distinguish "n < 20 but > 0" cells from "n = 0/0" cells, since they have qualitatively different statistical content.

3. **§2.4 line 79** — "homoscedastic Mondrian split-conformal regressor" is a slight misnomer: split-conformal with absolute-residual nonconformity is **distribution-free** but not strictly "homoscedastic"; the layer just doesn't *adapt* the interval width to local heteroscedasticity. Suggest replacing "homoscedastic Mondrian" with "constant-width Mondrian" or "absolute-residual Mondrian" throughout the manuscript, including the abstract.

4. **§3.5 line 197** — "78.3 squared distance units" — the unit is the squared Mahalanobis distance value on a 17-dim space; recommend annotating with "D²(x) = 78.3" and the implied effective degrees of freedom on the χ² comparison, since the parametric reference is χ²(17, 0.95) = 27.6.

5. **§4.5 line 286** — Zenodo DOI placeholder text "[Zenodo dataset DOI to be minted at submission — placeholder for 10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXX]" — for PMEA's editorial workflow, the DOI must be live (not minted) at the time of submission via ScholarOne, since the editor enforces a check before review assignment. Confirm with the editorial office that placeholders are acceptable at portal entry; if not, mint the DOI before submission.

6. **Cover letter line 23** — "twenty-four references" but the manuscript reference list has 23 entries (counting Aresti 2019 through Whinnery & Forster 2013). Recount.

7. **§1 line 25** — "Portela et al. (2025) recently demonstrated…" — first use; suggest naming the journal venue inline ("Portela, Banga & Matabuena 2025 in *PLOS Computational Biology*") so the reader can immediately calibrate the comparator's prestige and scope. The reference list does carry this.

8. **§2.4 line 73** — "**Relation to conformalized survival analysis.**" — this is correctly listed as a §4.6 deferral, but the §2.4 location is awkward: a reader expects the Methods to commit to a method, not flag a future deferral. Suggest moving this paragraph to §4.4 Limitations as a one-paragraph "Methodological deferral" subsection, leaving §2.4 to describe what was done.

9. **§3.2 line 145** — "The XGBoost monotonicity-constrained two-stage approach avoids this damping." — this is plausible but should reference the RF row in supplementary Table S1 by number; right now the comparison floats without a numerical anchor in the main text.

10. **§3.7 line 232** — the OSF amendment mapping rule `g_peak_abs = min(9.0, 1 + onset × real_mean)` is non-obvious. The 9.0 cap and the +1 baseline are arbitrary-looking unless explained; add a half-sentence rationale ("9.0 is the upper bound of the rapid-onset training envelope; +1 is the baseline 1-G plateau before onset begins"). Currently a reviewer must navigate to the amendment file to understand the mapping.

11. **§2.6 line 103** — "Saltelli sampling (`SALib.sample.saltelli.sample`) drove the surrogate (N = 1,024 base samples, D = 9 features, 20,480 evaluations)." For Saltelli sampling, total evaluations is `N × (2D + 2) = 1,024 × 20 = 20,480` for second-order indices, which matches. Confirm the formula explicitly so the reader can audit.

12. **§3.5 line 210** — "differences ≤ 0.04 AUROC" — the Mahalanobis vs Isolation Forest 0.04 AUROC ceiling on the LOGO folds is reported as evidence neither dominates; consider noting that 0.04 AUROC is itself below the per-fold bootstrap CI width on these fold sizes, so the comparison is power-limited.

## Strengths to preserve

1. **The additive-wrap architecture.** Preserving the FAA-validated Fortran binary byte-for-byte and adding the ML layer as a wraparound rather than a rewrite is the right structural choice for a regulatory-physiology paper. It separates the "validate the wrapper against the validated core" question (this paper) from the "validate the core against reality" question (paper-2). The §1 framing of this separation is clear and correct.

2. **The OOD calibration headline.** 0.953 conformal in-envelope rate on the held-out test split, within 0.3 pp of nominal, against a parametric χ²(17, 0.95) baseline that would flag 37.2 % of in-distribution rows as OOD — this is the cleanest result in the paper. The distribution-free conformal abstention layer is the right construction for the mixed-continuous/binary/ordinal feature space, and the manuscript correctly stops short of claiming Mahalanobis itself is Gaussian-valid.

3. **Honest reporting of `time_to_gloc_s` weakness.** The manuscript flags the regressor R² lower CI bound at −0.055, the Mondrian under-coverage at 0.861, and the n = 36 event-positive slice as the headline target's weakest empirical anchors. The H6 external-validation arm is reported as a "partial failure with a well-defined operational scope" rather than spun as a success. This is exactly the standard PMEA expects on a model-identification paper, and it should not be diluted in revision.

4. **The reproducibility package.** OSF pre-registration (with explicit amendment trail), Zenodo dataset deposit, Docker reproducibility image, 80 passing tests, deterministic figure rendering from committed data products, datasheets for datasets, model cards for the surrogate and OOD detector, TRIPOD-AI checklist. This is at the high end of what PMEA submissions deliver and should be preserved verbatim.

5. **The H6 archival validation arm.** Even on N = 8, the external arm is a genuinely useful piece of methodological honesty. It quantifies a CGEM-vs-reality discrepancy (rather than a wrapper-vs-CGEM discrepancy), which is the right framing. The "lower-bound at slow onset" operational interpretation in §4.4 line 251 is the defensible reading.

6. **Sobol/Morris dual-method screening with `g_peak_abs` × `profile_duration_s` as the dominant interaction.** The Sobol total-order ranking (`g_peak_abs` 0.876–0.942 across event-time targets; `dehydration_level` 1.005 on HLAP) is the operationally meaningful separation of countermeasure-vs-fluid-management interventions. Keep this section essentially as-is; the only addition is the H4a CI-width statement (Major Concern 10).

7. **The reference base.** Vovk et al. 2005, Boström et al. 2018, Romano et al. 2019, Candès et al. 2023, Gui et al. 2024, Davidov et al. 2025, Portela et al. 2025, Lundberg & Lee 2017 — the conformal/UQ reference base is current, accurate, and appropriate for a 2026 PMEA submission. The bibliography-correction trail in the OSF amendment (§F) is an additional reproducibility strength.

8. **Inference latency claim.** 50 µs vs 9 ms wall-clock is concrete, falsifiable, and operationally meaningful. The Saltelli-sweep enablement (20,480 evaluations in < 1 s vs ~3 min) is a clean illustration.

## Recommendation rationale

The manuscript matches PMEA's scope cleanly ("physiological modelling, simulation, model identification, and control" and "physics- and model-based machine learning"), the reproducibility package is well above average for the venue, the methodological delta vs the closest precedent (Portela, Banga & Matabuena 2025) is sufficient — Mondrian stratification, heteroscedastic CQR for the long-tailed event-time target, distribution-free conformal abstention over a robust-Mahalanobis OOD score, and a regulatory-aerospace demonstration substrate — and the OOD calibration result is genuinely strong. The author's honest reporting of `time_to_gloc_s` weakness and the H6 partial-failure framing are exactly what a PMEA reviewer should see on a model-identification paper. None of the methodological concerns rises to the level of a fundamental flaw.

However, four issues need a clean revision pass before the paper can be accepted: (1) the pre-registration chain on CQR is described in a way that overclaims blind locking and will not survive a careful read of the amendment file by a real reviewer (Major Concern 1); (2) the Mondrian/CQR stratification claim on `time_to_gloc_s` is effectively a single-stratum (military-ACM) result, and the abstract and §3.3 read this as a five-target Mondrian result, which it is not (Major Concern 2); (3) the abstract "within 4.6 pp on 4/5 targets" is not reproducible from Table 2 (Major Concern 3); and (4) the H6 N = 8 bootstrap CI is leverage-driven and a leave-one-out sensitivity is missing (Major Concern 4). The remaining majors (5–10) are second-order. These are all addressable in a revision without redoing experiments, so **Major Revisions** rather than Reject is the right call. The methodological foundation is sound; the manuscript needs honesty about the pre-registration chain, the single-stratum nature of the CQR comparison, and a small set of additional sensitivities to anchor the headline numbers.
